# Approximate solar kernel: source and contract

Production model is an adaptation of the **geometric** portion of `solarpos` in the official SSC repository (formerly NREL/ssc, redirected to NatLabRockies/ssc), not NREL SPA and not a claim of SPA precision. Retrieved through allowed GitHub access on 2026-10-25:

https://github.com/NatLabRockies/ssc/blob/c92cbf05e3fc1411a91c0fb9b48827dd2104c7f8/shared/lib_irradproc.cpp

Exact complete source is `lib_irradproc.cpp.source`, SHA-256 `2067a92386eb50e8dd9fe3f3148271ef03603d622bbb53f78051931c091b45b8`. Its BSD-3-Clause copyright, conditions, and disclaimer are preserved in that file. In particular: Copyright (c) Alliance for Energy Innovation, LLC. The SSC equations for mean longitude/anomaly, ecliptic longitude, obliquity, declination, right ascension and sidereal time are retained. UTC Unix timestamps replace the historical Julian-day bookkeeping. atan2 projections replace quadrant branches and the arbitrary vertical-ray azimuth. Refraction and the source's approximate sunrise formulas are deliberately omitted.

## Domain and units

Input coordinates are degrees: north-positive latitude [-90,90], east-positive longitude [-180,180]. Position accepts a Gregorian ISO instant with seconds and explicit Z or numeric offset. UTC years 1950–2050 are the deliberately restricted supported domain of this approximation (not a new accuracy claim made by SSC). Dates are interpreted using UTC milliseconds; leap seconds and UT1 corrections are unsupported. Output elevation, zenith and declination are **geometric geocentric degrees**; azimuth is clockwise from geographical north [0,360), null at geographical poles or a vertical ray (horizontal projection < 1e-10). Negative elevation is valid. No elevation, topocentric parallax, pressure, temperature, terrain horizon or atmospheric refraction corrections are applied.

This approximate model is suitable for educational estimates, not precision pointing/navigation. Tests compare seven independently computed pvlib SPA cases at <0.05° zenith and <0.1° azimuth, including 1950 and 2050, southern hemisphere, leap day and polar conditions. These are tested tolerances for the recorded cases, **not a uniform accuracy guarantee**, especially for azimuth near zenith. Published Reda/Andreas 2003 example apparent zenith is 50.111622° and azimuth 194.340241°; geometric zenith differs due to refraction and parallax. Production must not describe this algorithm as SPA.

## Sunrise/sunset contract

`sunEvents` finds rising/falling crossings of solar-center geometric elevation **-0.833°** (zenith 90.833°), a fixed conventional combination of mean solar semidiameter and near-horizon refraction. This is a convention, not a weather-dependent apparent elevation calculation. Horizon is level; observer elevation is zero; no terrain correction. Changing weather or altitude can materially change observed event times.

The date is YYYY-MM-DD in a **fixed offset** supplied as integer minutes east of UTC [-840,840], default zero. Search interval is [civil midnight, next civil midnight), translated to UTC; returned ISO timestamps include the actual UTC date. Named time zones and DST transitions are not modeled: the caller must supply an appropriate fixed offset, and 23/25-hour DST days require a different API. The entire requested half-open UTC interval must lie in [1950-01-01T00:00:00Z,2051-01-01T00:00:00Z); an explicit interval check rejects unsupported offset spillovers before searching. The exclusive upper endpoint is sampled at its preceding millisecond, so 2050-12-31 at UTC offset zero is supported. Either event can be absent on a transition day; returned arrays preserve all detected crossings and nullable singular fields give the first. No events plus a sampled minimum >0.15° above the threshold yields polar-day; a sampled maximum <-0.15° below it yields polar-night. Otherwise status is grazing: an unobserved crossing or horizon touch cannot be excluded. A one-minute bracket and bisection resolve crossings to <0.1 seconds numerically, but this is not physical accuracy. Extremely short grazing excursions between samples may not have resolved timestamps; the fail-closed grazing status prevents such an unresolved excursion from being described as continuous polar day/night. Events near polar transitions require a higher-fidelity model. Independent SPA roots using the identical -0.833° threshold agree within one minute in three recorded midlatitude fixtures, not a polar accuracy promise.

## Conservative sampling bound

The no-event classification uses a **0.15°** margin, not an empirical accuracy claim. From the pinned production equations, ecliptic longitude rate is at most `0.9856474 + (1.915 + 0.040) * pi/180 * 0.9856003 < 1.020` degrees/day; obliquity rate is 0.0000004 degrees/day. Equatorial direction angular speed is bounded by the sum of these rates. Sidereal rotation contributes `15*(0.0657098242/86400 + 1/3600) < 0.004179` degrees/second. Their sum is <0.0042 degrees/second, bounded conservatively by **0.005 degrees/second**. Elevation change cannot exceed direction angular distance. Every instant is within 30 seconds of a minute sample (the final sample is the last supported millisecond), yielding 0.15° maximum unsampled elevation change. Thus the conservative no-event status excludes a hidden crossing without assuming the extrema fall on minute boundaries. It can return grazing on some days that actually are continuous day/night; that loss of classification is intentional.

Deterministic regressions demonstrate both hidden noon excursions (2024-12-21, latitude 67.39769781352459°, longitude 0°) and hidden midday dips (2024-06-21, latitude 65.73296145375297°, longitude 180°), where consecutive minute samples have the same sign but an intermediate instant has the opposite sign.
